ABOUT

About me

My research interests lie in development, labor, public, population, and education economics. To explore these interests, I undertook an independent project through the Statistics Seminar at Syracuse University, examining the relationship between prolonged low fertility and the contribution of total factor productivity to economic growth. I am now developing this project into a draft paper while taking PhD-level Microeconomic Analysis.

I am an undergraduate student pursuing a B.A. in Economics and a B.S. in Mathematics at the University of Seoul, with graduation expected in June 2027, and am currently an exchange student at Syracuse University.

At Syracuse, I work as a research assistant with Professors Kristy Buzard and Shafaat Yar Khan on historical tariffs and trade, and with Professor Khan on reusable code for trade elasticity estimation. Through these projects, I am developing my skills in empirical research, econometric analysis, and reproducible research workflows.

Low Fertility, TFP, and Economic Growth:
A Panel Analysis

Jan–May 2026

Independent Research · Statistics Seminar, Syracuse University
Faculty Advisor: Jianxuan Liu

Using a panel of 41 low-fertility countries from 1970 to 2023, I examine the relationship between low-fertility duration and TFP's contribution to economic growth. The project combines fixed-effects regressions with Monte Carlo simulations based on a TFR Markov chain, kernel-smoothed transition probabilities, and LOOCV bandwidth selection.
